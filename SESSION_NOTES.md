Rather than guess-fix the sun's transform blind, went with what the existing geometry actually supports: a low-density `volumetric_fog` reveals the near-vertical sunlight as ambient haze/a soft column down the open stairwell top (where the walls end and the platform is open to the sky) instead of literally through the window. - **Shared foundation risk already covered:** the `main.tscn` double-lighting fix (see below) affects all three zones' baseline brightness/color, so judge village and tower's *new* saturation/glow against that corrected baseline, not against how they looked before this session.
- **Step 1 — fixed a real bug: double lighting.** `scenes/main.tscn` (the persistent root) had its own leftover `WorldEnvironment` + white `DirectionalLight3D` from Phase A that was never cleaned up, and it was stacking on top of every zone's own lighting at runtime (each zone also has its own Sun + WorldEnvironment). This was silently diluting every zone's mood, especially the shore's "dim cool light." Fix: `visible = false` on main.tscn's `Sun` (script never references it, confirmed safe). Main's `WorldEnvironment` left in place as a neutral fallback. **This fix affects every zone's lighting, not just the shore** — village and tower interior will likely read slightly differently (probably better/more saturated toward their intended color) even before their own dedicated pass.
- **Step 2 — shore atmosphere** (`scenes/zones/zone_02_shore.tscn`), the cinematic climax:
  - Volumetric fog added (density 0.05, cool albedo, temporal reprojection **disabled** — preemptively, to avoid a smear artifact during the Phantom Camera's instant priority-cut mid-cutscene) plus retuned flat fog (`fog_aerial_perspective`, `fog_sky_affect`) so the horizon disappears into mist per the zone doc ("endless water swallowed by mist").
  - `adjustment_enabled` + `adjustment_saturation = 0.7` for the "cold, desaturated" mood.
  - New shader `shaders/water_shimmer.gdshader` replaces the flat water material: animated swell (vertex sine waves), fresnel rim highlight, subtle animated specular. No texture assets.
  - New shared shader `shaders/soft_dot.gdshader` (billboarded soft-alpha circle, no texture) — reused by all future particle effects across zones. **Gotcha found and fixed:** `render_mode billboard` is NOT valid Godot 4 spatial-shader syntax (I misremembered) — billboarding needs manual `MODELVIEW_MATRIX` reconstruction in `vertex()` from `VIEW_MATRIX` + `INV_VIEW_MATRIX` basis columns. **Also learned: contrary to the plan's assumption, the headless GUT run DOES catch shader compile errors** (via resource loading when a test preloads a scene using that shader) — the dummy renderer parses/compiles shader code even though it can't render it. So `SHADER ERROR` in GUT/import output is a real, machine-catchable signal here, not something that only a human on a GPU can find. Only the visual *result* (does it look good) stays human-only.
  - New `Weather/Mist` `GPUParticles3D` node: sparse, slow-drifting mist wisps low over the water, using the shared soft-dot shader.
  - `Sun` DirectionalLight3D: energy 0.6→0.75 (compensating for the removed double-light), added `light_angular_distance = 1.8` for softer overcast-style shadows.
- **Verified (machine-only so far):** `--import` clean, 49/49 GUT tests, headless boot of `main.tscn` (real load path) clean, headless boot of `zone_02_shore.tscn` directly clean. **None of this proves it looks right** — fog density, water shader feel, particle density/drift, and the volumetric-fog/temporal-reprojection interaction with the instant camera cut are all human-eyeball-only.
